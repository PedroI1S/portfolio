Selected projects, 2022–2026

08 entries · open a row

No. Project Client Type Year
  1. (01) Harbour Index Client: Porto Tide Observatory Type: Data tool Year: 2026

    Forty years of tide-gauge readings drawn as rings, one per year. Researchers scrub decades in a second; the public version runs on a screen in the harbour master’s old office.

    Stack
    WebGL, DuckDB-Wasm
    Output
    Web app, wall screen
    Duration
    7 months
  2. (02) Kiln Variant Client: Faiança Rosa Type: Generative identity Year: 2026

    A ceramics brand whose logotype is redrawn from the temperature log of every firing. 312 firings so far: 312 marks, one silhouette you can still recognise across a shop.

    Stack
    Rust to Wasm, Figma plugin
    Output
    Identity generator, packaging
    Duration
    5 months
  3. (03) Low Frequency Atlas Client: Kurzwelle Archive Type: Installation Year: 2025

    Six pen plotters in a former transmitter hall drew the archive’s broadcasts as harmonograph traces, live, for the length of the show: 41 days and 2,300 sheets.

    Hardware
    6 pen plotters, 6 single-board computers
    Output
    Installation, print edition
    Duration
    41 days on site
  4. (04) Census Weave Client: Atlas Civic Lab Type: Data tool Year: 2025

    A tiled explorer for a national census where every tile is a parish and every curve a commuting flow. Built so a journalist on deadline finds a story in under a minute.

    Stack
    TypeScript, SVG, a lot of SQL
    Output
    Public explorer, embed kit
    Duration
    4 months
  5. (05) Seed Bank Client: Self-initiated Type: Plotter edition Year: 2024

    One stipple drawing a day for a leap year, each seeded by its date. 366 sheets of ink on cotton paper, and the whole archive can be plotted again from one line of code.

    Medium
    Pigment ink on 300 g cotton
    Format
    A4, 366 sheets
    Edition
    1 of 1 per seed
  6. (06) Granite Type Client: Pedreira do Norte Type: Generative identity Year: 2023

    A quarry’s identity built from the fracture lines of its own stone: a hatched display face, and a system that gives every block shipped its own mark.

    Stack
    Python, OpenType, Processing
    Output
    Typeface, marking system
    Duration
    6 months
  7. (07) Commuter Field Client: Linha Verde Transit Type: Data tool Year: 2023

    Two million trips a day rendered as a flow field, so planners can see where the network bends under load. It is now part of the operator’s Monday review.

    Stack
    Canvas 2D, Web Workers
    Output
    Internal tool, monthly prints
    Duration
    8 months
  8. (08) Murmur Wall Client: Festival Ruído Branco Type: Installation Year: 2022

    A 14-metre LED wall that listens to the room and answers in circles. Three nights, 11,000 visitors, no reboots.

    Hardware
    LED wall, 4 microphones
    Output
    Installation, live set
    Duration
    3 nights

Services

(02)

What we are hired for

05 disciplines

  1. (01)

    Generative identities

    Logos that are programs. We design the rules, write the generator and hand over a tool your team can run without us.

    Generator · brand toolkit · motion rules

  2. (02)

    Installations

    Plotters, LED walls and projection for lobbies, festivals and museums. Built to run unattended for years, not just for opening night.

    Concept · hardware spec · show control · upkeep

  3. (03)

    Data tools

    Explorable dashboards and explainers for research teams, newsrooms and archives. Accurate first, readable second, fast always.

    Prototype · production build · documentation

  4. (04)

    Plotter editions

    Pen-on-paper runs of 10 to 200 from any generator we have built, each sheet signed with the seed that made it.

    Edition design · plotting · certificates

  5. (05)

    Workshops

    Two-day intensives for design teams learning to think in systems. Bring laptops; leave with a working tool.

    In person · Porto or Berlin · up to 12 people

(04)

The studio

Porto / Berlin

Seedfile is three people, two cities and one six-pen plotter older than all of us. One designer who codes, one engineer who draws, one producer who does both. We start every job by writing the program, not the picture, so nothing we hand over is finished: change the seed and it draws again.
