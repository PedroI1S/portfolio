A continuous descent that gives ocean depth a physical scale.

3D / WebGL
web design · motion · interaction

Open live concept ↗

The signature interactionCross the waterline and scroll toward the seabed as depth, light and pressure change.

01 / The brief

The imagined subject

A factual editorial experience taking visitors from the ocean surface toward the Challenger Deep, with depth-related events, explanatory notes, and listed sources.

The visitor

General readers curious about the deep ocean, its scale and exploration.

The design question

How can a familiar scroll gesture make an unfamiliar depth understandable?

03 / Decisions that shape the experience

Decision / 01

Cross a real boundary in the scene

Pointer or tap rings disturb the title’s reflection. The plunge passes through a split view of air and water, with bubbles and an exposure change marking the transition.

Desktop capture

Decision / 02

Tie the reading to the descent

Native scroll controls depth, light, pressure, particles and projected labels. A chapter rail permits direct jumps while the continuous ocean keeps each section in spatial context.

Phone capture

Decision / 03

Keep the evidence in view

The deepest sequence leads into dive history, notes on approximate figures and a source list. The factual text remains readable as HTML, with a separate route to view the piece without 3D.

Desktop capture

04 / Scope & credits

Words, design and code by otherweather. The live Three.js r170 scene uses a water shader, points and fog, without images. Type: Jost and IBM Plex Mono. This factual editorial concept lists NOAA, WHOI, Smithsonian, scientific papers and other sources in the live page; figures are rounded. It is not affiliated with those institutions or expeditions.
